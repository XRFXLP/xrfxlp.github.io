I was building some anticipation of the journey ahead in the flight from Delhi to Leh, with very little sleep and sudden pangs of being stranded in the remote desert of Laddakh.

<!--more-->

### Arrival

I had a very little sleep as there was a 2.5hr layover duration of around 3-5AM at Delhi airport. Every once in a while I was getting sudden pangs of being stranded, deserted or lost in the remote desert of Laddakh. This was my first such trip where I was going to roam for a week with little touch of civilization.

I saw some foreigner but I was mildly interested in talking to those peeps, although I eventually gave in to a guy who was sitting around me. That guy was going to travel along with 4-5 cousins and sisters and they had everything booked from travel agency and those guys had prepared their itinerary. We talked a little bit about whereabouts and what these folks are doing in their life, then I told them that I didn't had any such itinerary and no bookings of any sorts. He did some warning on to be careful and all.

After a while sun came up and we were lost in the beauty of it. I saw the phenomenon of morning sun grazing over the glaciers for the first time in my life and it was beautiful.

![views from the airplane](PXL_20250911_004721611.jpg)

I heard a lot about the breath shortness upon landing at the Leh airport which was again the first time for me but I didn't noticed any significant difference for the first few minutes when we were escorted from airplane to airport via bus. I had a common cold from Bangalore from a week ago and medicines were not helping, I was expecting it to be worse in Laddakh and recovery even slower.

When I got out from airport, holding the only dufflebag and a backpack that I brought, I started feeling a sudden tiredness and weakness. I sorta knew that this is the strange effect that everyone was talking me about. I checked network on my SIM, and it was all emergency-calls only, something was not working and I expected otherwise — that meant I had to go all-cash and no internet until I get myself a local SIM.

Fortunately there was an ATM just outside of the airport exit gate and I got myself some cash enough to pay for the hotel and the cab that I was yet to find. I read somewhere that airport was close-by to the Leh town (3-4 km) even though cab drivers charge something like 500 for dropping you to the town. I didn't had to fight a lot before giving up on walking to the town and to get myself the soonest cab that I can get so I can lay down.

![views from the cab](PXL_20250911_015658282.jpg)

On the way to the mall road, I saw folks prepping for marathon, apparently highest altitude marathon on the world and I was genuinely impressed upon the grit of these people — lot of them were senior folks. I UPI'd the taxi driver using his hotspot and started looking a good place to sleep. I checked for a few hotels before settling in wooden house where there was a lovely Laddaki old lady as the caretaker.

The room had a cozy feeling about it and a big window overlooking backyard and snow capped mountains faraway.

![views from the hotel](PXL_20250911_062704200.jpg)

After few minutes of rest, I paid the woman rent for a night and went back for a nap after munching on few cookies that I brought from Bangalore.

I felt a little better when I woke up, and now I had two action items in mind: get a SIM and make UPI work somehow. I got out of the bed and immediately realized if I make sudden movements I would pass out in coma and I still have to limit the movements for proper acclimatization. I went to mall road by taking small steps and got myself a SIM, went back to hotel for another round of nap.

![mosque in mall road](PXL_20250911_080301365.RAW-01.COVER.jpg)


In evening I called home to tell that everything is fine and I got myself a nice place to stay. There was not much in the night, I dined at the place just outside of the hotel, and went back to sleep.


### Planning the journey ahead

On day 2, it was cold but I was feeling excited to get things going. I went for the shop that I searched the earlier day where the shop opening time was mentioned as 8:30 AM. The streets leading the way were narrow and looked something less visited. When I reached at the shop it was closed. I asked an old man who was sitting on a porch but he was someone who was just passing by and has a local business to attend to.

I went for a little stroll ahead of the pathway. The houses were built in a Tibetan architecture and were of pretty much the same colour, perhaps following the traditional colour convention. I noticed that in almost all of the houses, there were small gardens filled of apple trees with little flocks of apples on them. I took a bunch of photos as this was the first time I was seeing an apple tree with apples — up until now this was mostly a distant fruit who just happen to appear in the market.

![apple trees](PXL_20250912_025008790.jpg)

I followed the same street a bit more. There was a fast voluminous river stream with bluish water, felt like coming from all the way from the skies.

![river stream](PXL_20250912_025611693.MP.jpg)

I went back to look for other rental shops in the same street. I noticed handplastering on the walls of the houses, it was a beautiful sight to see.

![handplastered walls](PXL_20250912_030652516.jpg)

I found few shops that were renting out Himalayan 411s. I checked one and immediately rented it out and came back to my homestay for few packings and gearing up.

I started from my homestay after having a breakfast at the same shop as earlier, and started out my home to the Leh-Manali highway. After riding for 4-5 km the views became insanely beautiful and the roads were quite smooth for most of the part. The first monastery in which I arrived was Thiksey monastery, which was quite different from the monasteries that I've been in the past.
